What Working in F&B Taught Me About People
Thoughts | 30 Jul 2026

I used to think working in F&B was mostly about taste, technique, and timing. But after years behind the line and managing front-of-house service, I realized the kitchen was just a backdrop. What F&B really is, at its core, is an intense, crash course in human psychology. When people are hungry, tired, celebrating, or having the worst day of their lives, food is often where they bring those emotions. Watching guests interact with a menu—and with our staff—taught me more about human nature than any book ever could.

The biggest takeaway? Hunger strips away people’s filters, but kindness restores them just as fast. I’ve seen the most stressed, impatient customer completely soften after a warm greeting, a honest recommendation, and a hot plate of food served with genuine care. It made me realize that most of the hostility we encounter in daily life isn't actually about us—it’s usually about someone carrying an unseen weight. Food has this rare power to lower people's defenses, creating a brief window where complete strangers can connect over a shared experience.

This lesson leaked directly into how I handle creative work and relationships outside the kitchen. It taught me empathy, active listening, and the art of reading the room before saying a word. In creative projects, just like in service, you aren't designing or writing for yourself; you’re creating for a human being with real emotions, short attention spans, and a need to feel understood. F&B proved to me that no matter how good your product or art is, people will always remember how you made them feel long after they've forgotten the details.
