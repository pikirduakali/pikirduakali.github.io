Why I Stopped Trying to Optimize Everything
Thoughts | 30 Jul 2026

I used to be at a point where every single aspect of my life needed a system.

Balancing the F&B world with creative work constantly forced my brain into two extreme modes: the strict precision of kitchen recipes and the boundless exploration of content creation. Instead of finding balance, I became obsessed with efficiency. I tracked my sleep, used rigid time-blocking to design new menus, and picked coffee shops not for their brew, but for their Wi-Fi speed and outlet availability. Everything had to be optimized—until I realized I had just turned myself into an exhausted machine.

My turning point came right in the middle of a chaotic dinner service. I was so fixated on shaving a few seconds off prep time that I completely forgot to appreciate the buzz of the kitchen and the smiles of customers enjoying my food. The same thing happened with my creative projects: ideas born from "optimized" algorithmic frameworks felt bland and soulless. I was so focused on building the perfect workflow that I lost sight of why I fell in love with these two industries in the first place: <strong>spontaneity and flavor</strong>.

Now, I intentionally leave room for inefficiency. I let the kitchen get a little messy when experimenting with new ingredients, and I let my mind wander without a timer when brainstorming visual concepts. It turns out that the best ideas and most authentic recipes actually come from those moments of imperfection. Stopping the drive to optimize everything wasn't about giving up or getting lazy—it was about making space for the one thing that truly matters in both creative work and F&B: the <strong>human touch</strong>.
