Good Enough is Better Than Perfect
Thoughts | 30 Jul 2026

I used to treat "perfect" as the bare minimum. In the kitchen, that meant spiraling over a sauce that was a fraction of a degree off. In my creative work, it meant spending four extra hours tweaking color palettes that no client would ever consciously notice. I convinced myself that this relentless high bar was what separated good work from great work. In reality, it was just perfectionism wearing a mask of high standards, and it was draining every ounce of joy out of what I created.

The shift happened when I realized how much perfectionism was actually holding me back. Waiting for the "perfect" conditions meant holding onto menu concepts for months instead of letting real customers taste them and give feedback. It meant sitting on finished creative projects because I was terrified of a minor flaw. Perfectionism doesn't guarantee excellence—it just guarantees delay, burnout, and a deep fear of making mistakes. I was so focused on avoiding flaws that I stopped shipping work altogether.

Learning to embrace "good enough" wasn't a compromise on quality; it was a commitment to momentum. In F&B, a dish served warm, delicious, and on time creates a far better experience than a "perfect" dish that takes an hour to leave the line. In creative work, an authentic 90% solution released into the world beats a 100% solution trapped on a hard drive every single time. "Good enough" leaves room for trial, error, and actual growth. It turns out that done really is better than perfect, because done gets to live, adapt, and connect with real people.
